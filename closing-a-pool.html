Closing a Pool for Winter

What to actually drain, what to leave alone, and the step most guides skip entirely — the one that decides whether opening day next season is easy or ugly.

Why closing properly matters

A pool that's closed properly opens easily. A pool that's closed in a rush — chemicals a bit off, filter not cleaned, water level wrong — is the one we hear about in spring, usually as "why is this thing green" or "why won't the pump prime." None of the steps below are hard. Skipping any of them is what causes the problems.

  1. Give it one last clean

    Vacuum, brush, and skim thoroughly before you close up. Anything organic left in the water or on the walls — leaves, algae, debris — has all winter to break down and feed problems you'll find in spring.

  2. Balance the water one final time

    Test and adjust pH and alkalinity right before closing, not weeks before. Balanced water sits far more stable over winter than water that was balanced a month ago and has since drifted.

  3. Run a final backwash and filter cycle

    This is the step most guides skip. Run the filter and do a full backwash (or clean the cartridge) right before shutting everything down. A filter that goes into winter dirty spends the whole off-season with trapped debris breaking down inside it — which is a genuinely unpleasant thing to open up in spring, and can shorten the filter's life.

  4. Protect the equipment from freezing

    If you're anywhere that gets a hard freeze, drain the pump, filter, heater, and exposed plumbing, and add air pillows or plugs where recommended for your setup. Water left in equipment can freeze, expand, and crack housings — an expensive mistake for a five-minute job.

  5. Adjust the water level for your cover type

    Above-ground pools with an air pillow under the cover, and inground pools with a safety cover, generally want the water lowered somewhat below normal — check what your specific cover type needs, since this varies more than people expect.

  6. Fit the cover properly

    A loose or poorly secured cover lets in exactly the debris and sunlight you're trying to keep out, which defeats most of the point of covering the pool at all. Take the extra few minutes to get it properly tensioned and secured.

Climate matters more than any single step

How aggressively you need to winterize depends heavily on where you are. A hard-freeze climate needs real equipment draining and freeze protection; a mild climate might just need a good clean, balanced water, and a cover. If you're not sure which category you're in, that's worth checking before you decide how far to go with any of the above.
