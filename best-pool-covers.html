Best Pool Covers by Type & Size

Solar, thermal, safety and winter covers — matched to climate, pool shape and how hands-off you want maintenance to be.

What each cover type actually does

"Pool cover" covers a lot of genuinely different products, and picking the wrong category is the most common mistake we see — usually someone buying a solar cover expecting it to double as a safety cover, which it was never built to do.

Solar covers
Lightweight bubble-style covers that float on the surface, trap solar heat, and dramatically cut evaporation. Cheapest option, but not load-bearing and not built to survive winter in a freeze-prone climate.
Thermal covers
Similar heat-retention purpose to solar covers but usually heavier, more durable material — often chosen for pools used year-round in mild climates where a bubble cover would wear out too fast.
Safety covers
Mesh or solid covers rated and anchored to support real weight — the only category that should ever be relied on for child or pet safety. Cost more and are typically fitted for inground pools specifically.
Winter covers
Built to keep debris and sunlight out during the off-season, secured with cables, water bags, or anchors. Not necessarily load-bearing unless separately rated as a safety cover too.

Matching cover type to what actually matters to you

Most buying decisions come down to one priority overriding the others:

  • Hot climate, want to extend the swim season — a solar or thermal cover pays for itself fastest here.
  • Cold climate, closing up for the off-season — a proper winter cover is the right tool, not a repurposed solar cover.
  • Young kids or pets around the pool — a rated safety cover isn't optional, regardless of what else you also use day to day.
  • Water-scarce area, high evaporation losses — a solar cover's evaporation reduction is usually the single biggest lever available.

These aren't mutually exclusive — plenty of owners run a safety cover for security and a solar cover for daily heat retention, swapping between them.

What a cover actually saves

Evaporation is the single biggest source of both water loss and heat loss from an uncovered pool — more than conduction through the pool walls or overnight radiative cooling combined, in most climates. A well-fitted cover can cut evaporation by up to 95% while it's on. For a heated pool, that translates to real money: uncovered heated pools commonly cost an extra $50–100 a month in heating during swim season compared to a consistently covered one, on top of the water refill cost from evaporation itself. For an unheated pool, the savings show up mainly as less frequent topping-up and a few degrees of natural solar warming from a solar cover.

Manual vs roller systems

A manual cover is simply laid over the pool by hand and secured — the cheapest option, and genuinely fine for smaller pools or covers that go on and off infrequently. A roller system mounts the cover on a reel, letting you unroll and retract it in under a minute. Rollers cost more upfront but make daily use of a solar cover far more likely to actually happen — a cover that's a hassle to put on tends to get left off, which defeats the purpose. For anyone using a solar cover daily on a larger pool, a roller is worth the extra cost.

Getting the size right

Measure at the water's edge, not the surrounding deck or coping — this is the single most common sizing mistake. Check whether a listed size is the cover's finished size or an oversized blank meant to be trimmed to fit; many solar and winter covers are sold slightly larger than standard pool sizes specifically so they can be cut down, which is more forgiving than ordering undersized. Irregular inground shapes (kidney, freeform) typically need a custom-cut cover rather than an off-the-shelf round or rectangular one.

Common questions

Do I need a different cover for summer and winter?

Many owners use one cover type year-round, but a solar cover left on through winter in a freezing climate can be damaged by ice, and a heavy winter cover left on through summer blocks sun and does a job the pool doesn't need done. Matching cover to season gets the most life out of each.

Will a solar cover actually heat my pool, or just reduce evaporation?

Both. The bubble structure traps solar heat and transfers some of it into the water, typically raising temperature a few degrees over several sunny days, while also cutting evaporation — which is usually the bigger effect on both water loss and heat retention.

Can any cover double as a safety cover?

No — only covers specifically rated and anchored as safety covers should be relied on to support weight. A solar or standard winter cover can look solid but will not reliably hold a child or pet, and treating one as if it will is a genuine safety risk.

How do I know what size cover to order?

Measure the water's edge, not the deck or coping, and check whether the listed size is the cover's finished size or the size before trimming — many solar and winter covers are sold slightly oversized so they can be trimmed to an exact fit.
